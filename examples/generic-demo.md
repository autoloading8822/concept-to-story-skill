# Generic demo: the Saturday you cannot spend twice

## Knowledge Point 1 — Opportunity cost

### Source

**SOURCE: synthetic Markdown text** — [Small Decisions, Clear Structures, section 1](../tests/fixtures/learning-source.md#1-opportunity-cost), by Concept to Story contributors. No PDF page number applies. The options must be feasible and mutually exclusive; the relevant value is the best alternative forgone.

### Core Idea

When you choose one option, you give up the best other option you could actually have taken. That forgone value is the opportunity cost, not the sum of every possible thing you did not do.

### Why It Matters

You can notice why an activity with no ticket price may still be costly: it occupies time that could have served something you value more.

### Story

**SUPPLEMENTARY EXPLANATION — original fictional scenario.**

Maya has one free Saturday afternoon. She can earn $80 on an extra shift, visit her sister, or join a free cooking class. All three happen at the same time. She lists what each would give her. This week, an overdue visit matters most to her; the shift comes second because rent is close. She chooses the visit. At dinner, a friend says, “You lost $80 and a cooking class, so that visit was expensive.” Maya draws one empty square on her calendar. She could only have put one alternative into it. The visit displaced the shift she otherwise would have chosen, not a magical afternoon containing both the shift and the class. Next month, with different needs, her ranking might change.

### Memory Anchor

🧠 Memory Anchor: One calendar square that holds only one card.

### Back to the Theory

Maya is the decision maker; her single afternoon makes the options mutually exclusive. The conflict is between connection, income, and enjoyment. Her choice uses her current priorities. The missing shift is her best forgone alternative; the empty calendar square cues the constraint, not a claim that every value can be priced in dollars.

### Where the Analogy Breaks

Real options can sometimes be combined or rescheduled; then the feasible alternatives must be rebuilt. The story does not imply that money is the only measure of value, or that all rejected options should be added together.

### Real-Life Signal

Before agreeing to a commitment, name what would occupy that same time or resource if you declined. Check whether that alternative is actually available.

### Active Recall

You can use a spare room for either a quiet workspace or storage, and your preferred feasible alternative to the workspace is storage. A friend adds the value of a guest room that building rules do not allow. How would you identify the opportunity cost of choosing the workspace?
